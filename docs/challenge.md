# Challenge Brief: Codifying Design for Social Good

## Problem statement

How might BRIDGEGOOD codify and scale its Design for Social Good philosophy so students, alumni, mentors, volunteers, staff, partners, and outside communities can understand, practice, and carry it forward as the organization grows beyond any one person?

The goal is for its community to understand, practice, teach, improve, and carry forward the Design for Social Good mindset as the organization grows. Participants are encouraged to explore creative approaches beyond traditional training.

**Codifying** means making the values and reasoning behind BRIDGEGOOD's work explicit and shareable. **Success** means more people can explain how and why BRIDGEGOOD works and put that understanding into practice. The format and approach are open.

## 1. What is the problem, and why is it important?

Since 2009, BRIDGEGOOD's work has connected design with confidence, access, community, and economic mobility. Its philosophy helps students see themselves as creators, leaders, and problem-solvers. Its approach emphasizes:

- Designing with social impact and community benefit in mind.
- Helping people recognize the value of their lived experiences.
- Making space for voices that are often overlooked.
- Addressing identity, accessibility, discrimination, conflict, and inclusion directly.
- Preparing participants to become empathetic collaborators and problem-solvers.
- Building confidence, access, community, and economic mobility.
- Responding to participant feedback rather than prioritizing what is easiest for the organization.
- Helping people make informed career decisions instead of pushing everyone toward the same definition of success.
- Encouraging students and graduates to see themselves as creators, leaders, and problem-solvers.

BRIDGEGOOD wants more designers, technologists, educators, and community leaders to carry a thoughtful, inclusive, and community-centered mindset into the systems they influence. Today, people mostly experience this philosophy by taking part in BRIDGEGOOD programs directly. It passes on through conversations, mentorship, community relationships, and lived experience.

As BRIDGEGOOD grows, its values, teaching approach, and design mindset need to live beyond its small group of team members. The opportunity is to make the philosophy a shared foundation across students, alumni, mentors, volunteers, staff, and partners: something the community can use, improve, and carry forward.

**Connected bonus benefits** (teams are not expected to solve all three):

- Recruitment: helping candidates recognize their alignment with growth, community, creativity, and service.
- Alumni: carrying confidence, purpose, and Design for Social Good into workplaces and communities.

## 2. What is the current workaround or process?

BRIDGEGOOD currently shares and practices its philosophy through intensive, facilitator-led experiences:

- Conversations about identity, racism, discrimination, and workplace challenges.
- A social-worker-informed approach to conflict resolution.
- Accessibility education.
- Weekly reflections and participant feedback.
- Coaching and exposure to working professionals.
- Collaborative projects grounded in community needs.
- Curriculum and program changes based on each cohort's experiences.
- Direct relationships between participants, educators, coaches, mentors, alumni, and BRIDGEGOOD staff.
- Mentorship from the founder, core team, alumni, board members, and trusted community members.

This approach is highly personalized and effective. It is also difficult to scale, because it depends heavily on direct BRIDGEGOOD involvement and on people who already understand the culture being present.

The mindset is not yet consistently captured and shared in ways that students, alumni, mentors, volunteers, staff, and partners can use without depending on a small group to interpret it. The opportunity is to make the existing relational knowledge easier to understand and carry forward.

## 3. What would an ideal outcome look like?

The Design for Social Good philosophy becomes a shared organizational foundation that people can understand, practice, teach, improve, and carry forward. The organization can grow with integrity beyond any one person, and people outside its direct programs can also learn from and apply the mindset.

In an ideal future:

- People can recognize what distinguishes BRIDGEGOOD's philosophy from generic ideas about social impact, inclusion, or human-centered design.
- Students can understand BRIDGEGOOD's values before, during, and after participating in programs.
- Alumni can continue practicing the philosophy in portfolios, interviews, workplaces, mentorship, and community projects.
- Mentors and volunteers can support participants in a way that reflects BRIDGEGOOD's care, tone, expectations, and community-centered approach.
- Staff and partners can explain and practice the philosophy consistently without relying on the founder or a small leadership group each time.
- Designers, technologists, educators, organizations, and community leaders from different communities can learn from the mindset and adapt it responsibly in their own work.
- The philosophy continues to evolve through feedback from the people and communities using it.
- BRIDGEGOOD retains clear authorship, identity, and ownership of the philosophy as it scales.

Creative approaches beyond traditional curricula are welcome when they help people internalize the philosophy and carry it into action. The value lies in helping people understand how and why BRIDGEGOOD works and apply that understanding. A useful concept or prototype can create value during the hackathon without depending on guaranteed post-hackathon development.

## 4. Risks, guardrails, and special notes

- Think out of the box about how a philosophy or mindset can spread. A course is one possibility, but it should not be treated as the default or expected answer.
- Stay true to BRIDGEGOOD's mission and philosophy. Do not reduce the philosophy to a rigid checklist, slogans, branding language, or inspirational quotes.
- Do not become a generic marketing campaign.
- The founder's story is part of BRIDGEGOOD, but an archive of that story alone does not meet this challenge. The goal is a philosophy and mindset that the wider organization and community can practice and carry forward.
- "Social good," "inclusion," "access," and "human-centered" should have concrete meaning rather than functioning as vague branding language.
- Scaling should not remove the community voice, feedback, care, and lived experience that make BRIDGEGOOD's approach distinctive.
- Different communities may need to interpret and practice the philosophy differently.
- Content and experiences should follow inclusive-language and accessibility standards.
- Use only aggregated, anonymized, synthetic, or appropriately consented participant information.
- Do not use personal stories, identities, portfolios, artwork, or apprentice-created products without explicit permission.
- Any resulting concept should preserve BRIDGEGOOD's authorship, identity, and relationship to the philosophy.
- Technology must preserve human judgment and the relationships that make BRIDGEGOOD effective. Personal conversations, trust, encouragement, and context matter.
