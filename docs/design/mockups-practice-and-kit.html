“Can we skip the interviews?”

You're two weeks into a four-week sprint with a food pantry. The intake form confuses families, and many give up. The pantry director says: “We're short on time. My staff know what families need. Let's skip interviews and design from what we know.”

What would you do?

There's no score. Your choice stays private unless you share it.

Choose an option first.

How BRIDGEGOOD reasons about it

There isn't one right answer, but the reasoning matters. The pantry director knows a lot, and staff knowledge is real evidence. It's still secondhand. The prompts that matter here:

  • Are we solving the problem people actually have, or the problem we assume they have? (prompt 4)
  • Whose voice has not been heard yet? (prompt 3)
  • Are we choosing what is easiest for us or what is most useful for the community? (prompt 8)

BRIDGEGOOD teams have often chosen the small option: short conversations where families already are, with consent, and a volunteer interpreter when needed. It respects the deadline and the families. Option C protects the principle but can cost the partner's trust, and option A risks designing for staff instead of families.

Run a critique circle in your community

For teachers, community organizers and design leads who want to practice BRIDGEGOOD's Design for Social Good approach without BRIDGEGOOD staff in the room. You don't need to be a designer.

60 minutes4–12 peopleOne piece of work to discussPrinted prompt cards

The session

  1. 1
    Set up · 5 min

    Agree how you'll talk: be kind, credit others' ideas, and don't share anyone's personal story without permission.

  2. 2
    Share the work · 10 min

    Someone shows a flyer, a form, a website or a sketch. Say who it's for.

  3. 3
    Critique with the prompts · 30 min

    Draw three prompt cards. Talk through each one. Write down what you notice, not who's right.

  4. 4
    Decide one change · 10 min

    Agree on one change you'll test with the people the work is for.

  5. 5
    Tell BRIDGEGOOD · 5 min

    Share what you changed about the session, and what didn't fit your community.

Keep, and adapt

Keep (BRIDGEGOOD's core commitments): the people affected help shape the work · access from day one · credit people's ideas · no personal stories without permission.

Adapt freely: language, examples, timing, which prompts you use, where you meet.
